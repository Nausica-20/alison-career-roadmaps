[← Back to All Roadmaps](../README.md)

## 📋 Career Overview

**Data Scientists and Data Analysts** extract insights from complex data to drive business decisions and strategic planning. They transform raw data into actionable intelligence that helps organizations make informed decisions.

### Key Responsibilities
- Collect, clean, and analyze large datasets
- Create data visualizations and dashboards
- Build predictive models and statistical analyses
- Identify trends and patterns in data
- Communicate findings to stakeholders
- Develop automated reporting systems
- Collaborate with business teams
- Ensure data quality and accuracy

### Salary Expectations
- **Entry Level (Data Analyst):** $60,000 - $85,000/year
- **Mid Level:** $85,000 - $120,000/year
- **Senior Level (Data Scientist):** $120,000 - $170,000+/year

### Job Market Outlook
📈 **Excellent** - Data roles continue to grow as organizations become more data-driven.

## 💼 Career Progression Path

```
Data Analyst (0-2 years)
└─> Senior Data Analyst (2-4 years)
    └─> Data Scientist (4-6 years)
        └─> Senior Data Scientist (6-8 years)
            └─> Lead Data Scientist / Chief Data Officer
```

### Data Analyst
- Clean and analyze data
- Create reports and dashboards
- Support business decisions
- Learn tools and techniques

### Senior Data Analyst
- Lead analytical projects
- Develop analytical frameworks
- Mentor junior analysts
- Work directly with stakeholders

### Data Scientist
- Build predictive models
- Design experiments
- Drive strategic initiatives
- Communicate complex insights

### Senior Data Scientist
- Lead data science teams
- Set analytical strategy
- Influence business direction
- Drive innovation

## 💡 Tips for Success

### Learning Strategy
1. **Practice with Real Data** - Use actual datasets from the start
2. **Focus on Business Context** - Always connect analysis to business value
3. **Master One Tool at a Time** - Don't spread yourself too thin
4. **Build a Portfolio** - Document your projects publicly
5. **Network** - Connect with other data professionals

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid
- Neglecting data quality and cleaning
- Creating complex models without understanding basics
- Poor communication of findings
- Not understanding the business domain
- Ignoring visualization best practices
